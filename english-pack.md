# English Pack — self-presentation + survival phrases

> How to use: drill the self-intro out loud until automatic (5+ times, then record yourself once on your phone and listen back — painful but works). Phrases: pick 2–3 per week, use them in drill sessions until they come out without thinking. Русские глоссы — только чтобы быстрее запомнить, не для перевода в голове.

---

## 1. Self-intro ("Tell me about yourself") — 60–90 sec

Built from your real facts. Short sentences on purpose — easier to say under stress, and they sound confident, not simple.

> "I'm a senior frontend engineer with about ten years of experience — I've spent my career shipping product UIs. Right now I'm at Dats.Team, where I led a migration to the Next.js App Router and built internationalization for more than fifty countries.
>
> About a year ago I started building AI products in public. I shipped a full RAG pipeline — chunking, embeddings, retrieval, reranking — with an eval suite, and it's live on my site. Now I'm building a research agent in TypeScript on the Anthropic SDK: it takes a company name and produces a structured, fully-cited dossier.
>
> I'm looking for a product engineer role at a small AI startup — a place where my frontend depth and my AI engineering work are both useful from day one."

**30-second version** (recruiter screens, intros on calls):

> "Senior frontend engineer, ten years of shipping product UIs. For the last year I've been building AI products in public — a RAG pipeline with evals that's live on my site, and now a research agent on the Anthropic SDK. I'm looking for a product engineer role at an AI startup."

Rules: never say "career switcher", "learning AI", "transitioning". You are a senior engineer who **builds AI products**. Present tense, proof attached.

## 2. Three STAR stories (60–90 sec each)

STAR = Situation → Task → Action → Result. Не рассказ, а структура: 1–2 предложения на каждую букву.

### Story A — Technical depth ("Tell me about a hard problem you solved")

> **S**: "At Dats.Team we had a large Next.js app on the old Pages Router, and we needed to serve localized content for over fifty countries."
> **T**: "I owned the migration to the App Router and the whole internationalization layer."
> **A**: "I planned an incremental migration so the product kept shipping — route by route, with next-intl for translations, SWR for client caching, and build-time optimizations so fifty locales didn't explode our build."
> **R**: "We migrated without freezing feature work, and the i18n system now serves fifty-plus countries from one codebase. The pattern I used — incremental migration under live traffic — is the same muscle I now use for AI systems: ship small, measure, keep moving."

### Story B — The pivot ("Why are you moving from frontend to AI?")

> "Honestly? I remember how frontend felt ten years ago — every problem was new, and I was building things that hadn't existed before. Today most frontend work is assembling known patterns, and AI tools are automating exactly that part. Meanwhile, building with LLMs feels like frontend felt in 2016: young ecosystem, real engineering problems, no established playbook. So I didn't wait — I started shipping. My RAG project is live with a nine-out-of-nine eval score, my research agent is in progress, everything is open source. I'm not running away from frontend — I'm bringing ten years of product engineering into the part of the stack where it matters most right now."

(Это твоя же мысль — "аишка это новый фронтенд" — превращённая в интервью-ответ. Facts, не "passion".)

### Story C — Failure / learning (draft — needs one real fact from you)

Skeleton to fill together in a drill session. Good candidates from your history:
- A production incident or a bad deploy you owned and fixed (any of the 8 companies)
- The Formind performance work: what was broken, what you measured, bundle −25%, load +30%
- The 90-day pivot pause itself: planned a sprint, life broke it, redesigned the system to be sustainable — honest and memorable, shows self-awareness (use only as backup — tech story is safer first)

Structure: what went wrong → what you did → what you changed permanently so it can't happen again.

## 3. Survival phrase bank

### Buying time (выиграть 5 секунд — это нормально и звучит профессионально)
- "That's a good question — let me think for a second." *(дай подумать)*
- "Let me structure my answer." *(соберу мысли в структуру)*
- "So, if I understand correctly, you're asking about…" *(перефразируй вопрос — это и время, и проверка)*

### Clarifying (уточнить — интервьюеры это ЛЮБЯТ)
- "Just to make sure I understand — do you mean X or Y?"
- "Can I ask a clarifying question before I answer?"
- "What's the scale we're talking about — hundreds of users or millions?"

### Structuring (каркас ответа)
- "There are two parts to this: first… second…"
- "The short answer is X. The longer answer is…"
- "Let me start with the trade-offs." *(trade-off = компромисс между вариантами; это слово — валюта интервью)*

### Honest unknowns (незнание, поданное как сила)
- "I haven't used X in production, but I'd approach it like this…"
- "I don't know the exact answer — here's how I would find out."
- "I've read about it but haven't built with it yet. My understanding is…"

### Recovering (когда запутался — все путаются)
- "Sorry, let me rephrase that." *(переформулирую)*
- "Actually, let me correct myself — I said X, but it's Y."
- "I lost my thread for a moment — the key point is…" *(потерял мысль — бывает у нейтивов постоянно)*

### Talking about your projects
- "I built it end-to-end: from chunking and embeddings to retrieval and reranking."
- "Every claim the agent makes has to have a source URL — I enforce citations in the pipeline."
- "I wrote evals from day one, so I can prove it works, not just claim it."
- "The trade-off I chose was X over Y, because…"

### Your questions to them (готовь 3 на каждую компанию)
- "How do you evaluate the quality of your AI features — do you have an eval suite?"
- "What does the roadmap look like for the next six months?"
- "What would a strong first ninety days look like in this role?"

### Logistics & money
- "I'm based in Buenos Aires — UTC minus three, so I overlap almost fully with US East Coast hours."
- "My expectation is in the range of X to Y, depending on the overall package." *(не называй число первым, если можешь: "What range do you have budgeted for the role?")*
- "I'm currently employed, so I'd need about X weeks for a proper handover." *(handover = передача дел)*

### Closing a call
- "Thanks — I enjoyed this conversation. What are the next steps?"
- "Is there anything about my background I can clarify?"

## 4. Delivery notes (once, then forget)

- Slow is confident. Rushed is nervous. Your instinct under stress will be to speed up — do the opposite.
- Your accent is fine. Nobody hires for accent; they hire for clear thinking. Structure > pronunciation.
- Pauses are power. "Let me think" + 3 seconds of silence reads as senior, not as weak English.
- End answers with a landing sentence ("…so that's why I chose X"), not a trailing "…yeah, so…". Приземляй ответ.
